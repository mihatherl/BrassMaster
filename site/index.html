Brass Master

Sight-reading and fingering practice for brass players. Free, in your browser, and it works offline.

Start practising

No account, no install, nothing to pay.

What it does

It puts music in front of you at a tempo you choose, with a conductor beating time and a reference tone in your ear, and tells you honestly whether you played it.

The music is generated, so you are always reading something you have not seen before — which is the thing that actually makes a reader fluent. Play along on the valve buttons and every note is judged: right, wrong, or missed.

Every valved brass instrument

B♭ cornet and trumpet, flugel horn, E♭ tenor horn, B♭ baritone, euphonium, and E♭ or B♭ bass. Fingerings follow the instrument you pick.

Treble or bass clef

Read in whichever your part is written in, with the brass band treble-clef convention where it applies.

Scales, arpeggios and tunes

Drill a scale or an arpeggio in any key, read generated phrases, or play through written themes.

The band around you

Metronome, conductor, tempo dial, key dial and a reference tone — so there is something to play against, not just something to look at.

Who it is for

Any brass learner — beginner or advanced, in a band or not. It is built by a learner in a brass band, so the conductor and the key dial show their origins, but nothing about it assumes you play in one.

Fingering charts, without the chart

If you came looking for a tuba, euphonium, cornet or tenor horn fingering chart: this shows you the fingering under the note you are actually trying to play, and then stops showing you the ones you already know. Turn the hints off when you no longer need them.

On your phone

It runs in a phone browser and can be added to your home screen, where it works with no connection at all.

What is coming

Everything above is free and stays free. A paid Android app is being built, and it adds the things that need more than a web page can do:

  • Microphone mode — it listens to you play, instead of watching you press buttons.
  • Guided practice — goals, a planned session, and a progression that remembers between sittings and tells you whether you are actually improving.
  • Your own music — import a part and read it with everything else the app can do to it.

After that, playing your part with the other instruments around it, so you are reading in context rather than alone. An iOS version comes after the Android one.

Tell me what is wrong with it

This is built by one person, and the fastest way it gets better is people saying what broke or what they wish it did. Bugs, an instrument that is missing, something that felt wrong to read — all welcome at feedback@brassmaster.net.
